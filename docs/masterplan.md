1. How to use this

The group is four brands plus a corporate parent, each built in its own chat. This document is the shared spine: what is decided, what is open, what exists, and in what order things happen.

Rules for keeping it useful

Section 3 (Decision Log) is authoritative. To revisit a decision, re-record it here rather than changing it silently in a chat.
Section 12 (Open Decisions) is the shared to-do. Items move up into section 3 when settled.
Section 13 describes live code and goes stale fastest. Verify against the repo before relying on it.
File naming convention for any summary or handoff: NAME-YYYYMMDD-HHMM.md, Germany time zone. This replaces the older YYYYMMDD-HHMM_NAME form and the per-repo gtmwizards-website_<date>-<n> form.

5. accessDACH

Section 5 was substantially revised on 28 August 2026 against the PriceLabs proposal, PriceLabs_Kadanco_DACH_Proposal-20260828-1855.docx. That document is the first time this proposition was put in front of a real buyer with money. Read the caveat at the end of this section before treating any of it as final.

What it sells. A programme to enter and then grow in the German-speaking market (Germany, Austria, Switzerland), executed by operators who do the work themselves.

Who buys it. "DACH" is a word used by people outside the German-speaking market who want in. A founder in Stuttgart does not say "we operate in DACH". So the buyer is a US, UK, Nordic or Israeli B2B company. That much was already known and is confirmed. What was wrong was assuming they have no presence yet.

The first live prospect had already entered. They held roughly 12% of one German segment, around 1% of another, and a handful of enterprise accounts won on relationships with nothing organised behind them. They had also already staffed local teams in three other countries, so they knew how expansion works and were not looking to be taught.

Two consequences follow (D30):

The pitch is never "you should enter DACH". By the time a buyer talks to us, that decision is made and often already acted on. Any page that argues for the market insults the reader. The site sells execution and local knowledge, and it should say plainly that the case for the market is the client's own.
The plateau is the opening, and the blank page is the rarer case. The most common shape is a company with early German traction it cannot explain or repeat, usually because everything was translated from English and nobody in the company reads German. That is a more specific and more urgent problem than "we are thinking about Germany".

Two things from the earlier draft of this section survive unchanged:

The site is English-first and probably English-only. The buyer does not read German. German is the product, and the site language is English. This kills the /de/ and de-du migration work for this property, which is a large saving.
The glossary gets a spine. European Market Entry (16 terms) and Compliance & Privacy (16 terms) stop being filler. For a foreign company entering Germany, GDPR and regulatory fear is a top-three objection. The PriceLabs conversation confirmed this directly.
The five workstreams (D32)

Four are the initial engagement, the fifth is held for later:

Localisation. Audit of everything a German customer sees, glossary and register decision, main pages rewritten in German from scratch, terminology that matches how the market speaks, and the trust and compliance details German buyers look for.
Events. Research and shortlist the German-speaking industry events, recommend, book and negotiate, run the logistics, capture leads and follow up. Owned formats such as operator roundtables where those beat a stand.
Influencers and creators. German-language communication and day-to-day management of relationships the client has chosen. Selection and contracting stay with the client. This boundary matters and was set by the buyer, not by us.
Direction of an existing SEM and SMM agency. German keyword structure, a brief the agency can work from, periodic review of the German-language work, and landing pages written for German buyers.
Enterprise outbound, held as a later stage. The reasons are sequencing and knowledge build-up: sequences written before we know the market and the product are guesswork, the other four workstreams produce the glossary and messaging the sequences would use, and setting outbound up carries its own lead time. German rules on unsolicited commercial email (UWG §7) are a further point, and not the main one.

The phases are 0 analyse and diagnose (weeks 1 to 2), 1 execute (weeks 3 to 6), 2 expand (weeks 6 to 12), 3 iterate (week 13 onwards). Phases 0 to 2 fill the first quarter, which lines up with the minimum term in D31.

Phase 0 is co-owned and it is the thing being sold first. Competitor and substitute mapping, channel mix, an events shortlist, seasonality and demand patterns, the register decision, and the creator landscape. We do the work and bring the German read; the client brings product knowledge, existing customer data and commercial context. This is worth a page of its own on the site, because it is concrete, it is cheap to say yes to, and it is the only part of the offer a strategy consultancy cannot copy by rewriting a homepage.

What the site has to say, in priority order

These are the arguments that did the work in front of a real buyer, ranked by how much:

Operators do the work. "The standard agency model sells you the operator and staffs you the junior." This is the sharpest line the group has produced and it belongs on the homepage. It also makes the rate defensible: the fee buys the people in the room. Consistent with D9.
Direction of an agency the client already pays. None of the competitors listed below sells this. It is unusual, it is immediately understandable, and it answers the "we already have an agency" objection before it is raised.
True localisation as distinct from translation. The buyer used those words unprompted. The du and Sie decision, terminology, and rebuilding the proof around German channels and operators make it concrete.
The trust and compliance layer. Impressum, AVV, data handling, EUR invoicing and a USt-IdNr. These are the objections that lose mid-market and enterprise deals quietly. Naming them signals local knowledge faster than any claim about experience.
Systems written as we go, so the client owns the work from day one.
Vendor introductions at no fee (D34), with the point that no vendor can buy their way onto our list.

Caveat: this is one prospect

D30 to D34 come from one proposal for one prospect in one category. PriceLabs sells revenue management software to short-term rental hosts and property managers, which has its own channels, events and seasonality.

Safe to generalise: the buyer sits outside the German-speaking market (already known, now confirmed); the buyer often has traction already (D30, the genuinely new finding); localisation, events, creators and agency direction are what a real buyer asked for, and outbound was not; the compliance and trust objections are real and specific.

Not safe to generalise until a second and third conversation: that these five workstreams suit every buyer, since a hardware or fintech company entering DACH may want none of the creator work; that events matter this much, because they matter enormously in short-term rentals and may matter little elsewhere; the exact phase lengths.

Two live conflicts with the 26 August prototype. The homepage prototype sells "positioning, content, outbound and events" and names its four phases Positioning, Founder-led content, Outbound, Events and conversion, which came from the inherited use cases under D25. The PriceLabs proposal holds outbound for a later stage (D32) and names its phases analyse, execute, expand, iterate. Both cannot ship. See O24 and O25, and settle them before the site is built, because the Bar graphic encodes the phase sequence and changing the phases means redrawing it.

Working rule. Treat D30, D31 and D34 as settled and hold D32 loosely. If the next two prospect conversations ask for a different set of workstreams, the site should sell the phase 0 process and the operator model, with the workstreams presented as examples instead of a fixed menu.

11. Shared standards
Voice
No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere. Commas, colons, periods, parentheses. Write ranges out ("days 1 to 2", "5 to 50 properties"). Ordinary hyphens in compound words are fine and always were.
No contrarian "X, not Y" headlines or sentences. This was previously recommended here. It is now banned (D29). It is the single clearest sign that a page was drafted by an AI and shipped without editing. It appears as headlines ("Three demand systems, not one"), as sentence openers ("This matters commercially, not just editorially"), and as mid-sentence asides ("transcreation, not translation, of the core pages"). All three forms are out. If there is a real contrast worth drawing, write two sentences.
"Rather than" is the same tic wearing a different coat. Use it only for a genuine comparison between two named options. Never as a connector.
Plain words. If a shorter, more ordinary word exists, use it. Banned: motions, system of action, twelve motions one engine, compounds, legible, demonstrable, upweight, second-order, defensible, transcreation, greenfield, masthead, cadence, modular, forkable, ecosystem, proof surface, target universe, leverage (as a noun), failure mode, institutional knowledge, simultaneously, delve, robust, holistic, granular, seamless, landscape, underscore, pivotal, tapestry.
Industry vocabulary is not jargon. German terms the buyer uses daily (Ferienwohnung, Impressum, AVV, Zweckentfremdung, du/Sie) and trade metrics the buyer works with (ADR, RevPAR, ICP, ACV) stay. The test is whether the reader uses the word at work. Length is beside the point.
Benefit-led and concrete. Short sentences.
Open with what the reader gets. Do not open with a category label.
No invented metrics, testimonials or scale. Honesty is the edge. This is a two-person firm and the pages must be survivable in a sales call.
Reference sites: growigami.com (toward), accelitymarketing.com (away). This is contested. The accessDACH chat used Accelity as the model for its shape mechanic while rejecting its voice. The split is defensible, and per the rule in section 1 it should be recorded here rather than changed quietly. See O26.

One note on this document. Several rows in the section 3 decision log were written before D29 and still use the banned construction ("Operators", not "senior operators"; four brands plus corporate, not three). They are left as written because the decision log is a historical record. Do not copy the rhythm out of them into site copy.

How to check your own draft. Search it for: an em dash, an en dash, the string ", not ", the string "rather than", and each banned word. Every hit needs a rewrite. A word swap is not enough, because trading one dash for another punctuation mark leaves the same machine rhythm in place.

14. Hard-won gotchas, do not rediscover these
CSS minifier. Astro 7 minifies with Lightning CSS, which strips the unprefixed backdrop-filter and keeps only -webkit-. That silently kills nav blur in Firefox. Fix with vite: { build: { cssMinify: 'esbuild' } }.
Node version. Astro 7 requires Node >= 22.12. Cloudflare Pages defaults lower, so .nvmrc containing 22 is mandatory, with NODE_VERSION=22 in the Pages env vars as a fallback.
Sitemap version pin. @astrojs/sitemap 3.7.x requires Astro 5+. On Astro 4 it fails with Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'reduce').
Cloudflare injects its own robots.txt. A managed block with Content-Signal directives and Disallow: / for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot and Google-Extended is prepended at the edge. Disable it in the dashboard (Security, Bots, AI Scrapers and Crawlers, plus the Content Signals Policy) or it silently blocks the crawlers llms.txt exists to serve.
Astro i18n fallback generates redirect stubs for every untranslated locale path. Exclude them from the sitemap with a filter, or hundreds of thin URLs get submitted.
de-du is not a valid BCP-47 tag. Never emit it as hreflang; canonical those pages back to /de/ and keep them out of the sitemap.
Open Graph needs absolute image URLs. An asset helper that depends on an env var will emit a relative path when the var is unset, which is silently invalid. Resolve through new URL(..., Astro.site).
<html lang> must follow the locale. It was hardcoded to en on the German and Dutch routes on gtmWizards until 23 August 2026. Check this on every property.
Never load a JS library from a CDN. Both sites self-host fonts for GDPR reasons; pulling a library from jsdelivr hands visitor IPs to a US CDN and quietly undoes that. Bundle from npm.
R2 path quirk on the Kadanco bucket. Founder photos and the OG image sit under assets.kadanco.com/public/..., logos and badges do not. Do not repeat this on new buckets.
Founder photos were once expiring LinkedIn CDN URLs. They are on R2 now. Do not reintroduce media.licdn.com links.
Word puts em dashes back. Word's AutoFormat silently converts word - word into an em dash while you type, so a document that passed a dash check can fail again after one round of edits. Turn it off before editing any client document: File, Options, Proofing, AutoCorrect Options, AutoFormat As You Type, untick "Hyphens (--) with dash". The same setting exists separately in Google Docs under Tools, Preferences, Substitution.
